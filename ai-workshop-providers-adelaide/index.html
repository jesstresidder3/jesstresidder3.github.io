How to choose

Which one is worth your money

The right pick depends on your headcount, your budget, and whether you want a course or a build. Here is how I would sort it if you rang me and asked.

Under 20 staff, based in SA
Start with the government program

Nine hours of tailored support for $150 is not a price any private provider can meet, including me. Apply through simplefy.ai. If you finish it and want to go further than the pilot covers, that is the point to call someone privately.

One person, no team
Buy a seat at a public course

I do not run public ticketed courses, so if you are one person who wants to sit in a room next month and learn, Team 400 will serve you better than I will. A seat costs less than a private session and you get a scheduled date.

Already paying for Microsoft 365
Go to a Copilot specialist

If your organisation has bought Copilot licences and the real question is why nobody uses them, a Copilot specific session will get further than a general AI workshop. Hypergen run these in Adelaide.

A team with real tasks to fix
Get a session built around your work

This is the one I do. Everyone brings a laptop and the task that eats their week, and we build the thing that handles it during the session. Nobody leaves holding slides about the future of work with no tool set up.

Method

How I put this together

Every price on this page came off the provider's own page, or the South Australian Government program page, on 4 August 2026. None of it came from a directory or a summary. Figures are quoted the way each provider states them, so some carry GST and some do not, and the table says which.

I included a provider when I could confirm two things on their own site: that they deliver in Adelaide, and what the session involves. I excluded the ones whose Adelaide offer I could not confirm, which is why this list runs to six rather than a dozen.

I run one of the six, which is a conflict, and you should read that row with it in mind. The government program sits at the top of the table rather than buried underneath me because for a business with fewer than 20 staff it is better value than anything I sell.

Prices move. If you are reading this months from now, check the figure on the provider's own page before you budget against it.

Questions

Common questions about AI training in Adelaide

How much does an AI workshop cost in Adelaide?

Published Adelaide prices in August 2026 run from $150 for the full nine hour subsidised government program, through $799 + GST for a public half day seat, to $1,800 + GST for a private half day for up to five people. Providers running longer private corporate sessions quote on scope and mostly do not publish a figure.

Is there subsidised AI training for South Australian businesses?

Yes. The AI Capability Pilot Program is funded by the South Australian Government's Office for Small and Family Business and delivered by simplefy.ai. Businesses with fewer than 20 full time staff get a four hour build day for $50 per participant and five hours of one-on-one coaching for $100 per participant, plus six months of community access. Applications are open and the program runs across 2026 and 2027.

Do we need to be technical to get anything out of a workshop?

No. If you can send an email and use Google, you can do this. No coding is taught in my sessions and none is required. The people who get the most out of a workshop tend to be the ones who can name the repetitive part of their own week, which has nothing to do with technical skill.

How long should the session be?

One to two hours gets everyone set up properly and one workflow built. A half day covers several workflows and connects AI to the tools the team already uses, like Gmail, the CRM and the calendar. A full day is the advanced end, with agents that run a whole task start to finish. Tell me what your team does manually every week and I will tell you which length is worth paying for.

Can workshops run outside Adelaide or online?

Mine do. In person across Adelaide and regional South Australia, travel for client sessions in Victoria and New South Wales, and online for teams anywhere in Australia. The online version is still hands-on, so everyone still builds something on their own laptop.

Will AI training mean we need fewer people?

That is not what I build and it is not what I sell. The workflows give a team back the hours they lose to research, admin and follow up, so those hours go into the work only a person can do. Every session I run assumes the same people are still there afterwards.
